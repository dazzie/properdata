## Key metrics definitions

Before the milestones, clarity on what we're measuring:

| Metric | Definition | Target benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| **MRR** | Monthly Recurring Revenue. Sum of all active paid subscriptions normalised to monthly. Annual subscribers counted as annual price ÷ 12. | — |
| **ARR** | Annual Recurring Revenue. MRR × 12. | — |
| **Free subscribers** | Email subscribers receiving the free weekly pulse via Substack. | — |
| **Paid subscribers** | Subscribers paying for Insider or Professional tier. | — |
| **Free-to-paid conversion** | % of free subscribers who convert to paid. | 5-8% (niche newsletter benchmark) |
| **Monthly churn** | % of paid subscribers who cancel in a given month. | <5% monthly (target <3% by Month 12) |
| **Annual billing %** | % of paid subscribers on annual vs monthly billing. | >55% (annual subscribers churn at 1/3 the rate of monthly) |
| **ARPU** | Average Revenue Per User (paid). Blended across Insider and Pro tiers. | €18-22 blended |
| **LTV** | Lifetime Value. ARPU ÷ monthly churn rate. | >€300 |
| **CAC** | Customer Acquisition Cost. Total marketing spend ÷ new paid subscribers acquired. | <€30 (organic channels) |
| **LTV:CAC** | Ratio of lifetime value to acquisition cost. | >3:1 (target 8:1+ with organic) |
| **CAC payback** | Months to recover acquisition cost from a subscriber. ARPU ÷ CAC. | <6 months |
| **Email open rate** | % of free subscribers who open the weekly pulse email. | >40% (niche benchmark; mass newsletters average 20-25%) |
| **Email click rate** | % of openers who click a link in the email. | >8% |
| **NRR** | Net Revenue Retention. Revenue from existing subscribers this month vs same cohort last month, including upgrades and churn. >100% means expansion exceeds churn. | >100% by Month 12 |
| **Gross margin** | (Revenue - infrastructure costs) ÷ Revenue. | >85% (SaaS benchmark: 78% median) |
| **Burn multiple** | Net burn ÷ net new ARR. How much you spend to generate each dollar of new ARR. | <2x (efficient); <1x (excellent) |

## Cohort analysis framework

Track every monthly cohort independently. This is the most important analytical discipline for the business.

| Cohort | Size at signup | 30-day retention | 90-day retention | 180-day retention | 12-month retention |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Month 4 (founding members) | 20 | 95% | 90% | 85% | 75% |
| Month 5 | 20 | 90% | 82% | 70% | 60% |
| Month 6 | 30 | 88% | 78% | 65% | 55% |
| Month 7+ | — | 85%+ | 75%+ | 62%+ | 50%+ |

**What to look for:**
- **Later cohorts should retain better than earlier cohorts.** If Month 10 cohort retains at 80% after 90 days vs Month 6 cohort at 70%, your product, content, and onboarding are improving. If later cohorts retain worse, something is degrading.
- **The 90-day cliff.** Industry data shows 60-70% of annual churn happens in the first 90 days. If subscribers survive 90 days, they're likely to stay for 12+ months. Your onboarding and first-month experience is the single biggest retention lever.
- **Annual billing cohorts.** Track annual subscribers separately. They should show dramatically better retention than monthly (1/3 the churn rate is the benchmark).
- **Persona-based cohorts.** Track investor personas separately from buyer personas. Investors should retain 2-3x longer (perpetual need vs lifecycle need).

## What kills the business — early warning signals

| Signal | What it looks like | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Content fatigue** | Open rate drops below 30%. Unsubscribe rate exceeds 1% per email. | Refresh content format. Survey subscribers. Test different angles. |
| **Conversion plateau** | Free-to-paid stuck below 3% after 3 months of paid tier. | Paywall positioning is wrong. Test: grant calculator behind paywall, different preview length, different CTA copy. |
| **Churn spiral** | Monthly churn exceeds 8% for 3 consecutive months. | Product-market fit is failing. Exit surveys. The most common reason: "I found what I needed and left" (lifecycle churn, hard to prevent) or "the content wasn't worth the price" (value gap, fixable). |
| **Pro tier rejection** | Fewer than 5 Pro subscribers after 3 months of outreach. | The professional value proposition isn't landing. Change the offering: different deliverables, different pricing, different target (try mortgage brokers instead of auctioneers). |
| **Solo operator burnout** | Weekly cadence breaks. Quality drops. Subscriber queries go unanswered. | The most likely failure mode. Hire sooner (even at a loss) or reduce scope (fewer towns, less frequent publication). The agentic pipeline is designed to prevent this, but editorial quality requires human attention. |
| **Data pipeline failures** | PPR ingestion breaks. BER data stale. Planning tracker gaps. | Technical debt. Allocate 20% of time to pipeline maintenance. Sentry alerts are the early warning system. |
| **Regulatory intervention** | DPC complaint about PPR data processing. Central Bank query about financial advice. | Unlikely if compliance foundations are in place. Respond promptly, engage solicitor, adjust product positioning if needed. |

## The honest bottom line

At €15/month infrastructure cost, the business is cash-flow positive from subscriber #8. The risk-adjusted expected outcome over 24 months is an ARR of €100-300K — a viable solo or two-person business generating meaningful income from a product that genuinely helps people make better property decisions.

The three metrics that determine success or failure:
1. **Free-to-paid conversion rate.** If this exceeds 5%, the business works. If it stays below 3%, it doesn't.
2. **Monthly churn.** If this stays below 5%, the LTV math works. If it exceeds 8%, the business is a leaky bucket.
3. **NRR.** If this exceeds 100% by Month 12, the business compounds. If it stays below 95%, growth requires ever-increasing acquisition spend.

Everything else — ARR, subscriber count, town coverage, feature set — is downstream of these three numbers.
