# ProperData — Persona Types

**Date:** April 2026

## The market in numbers

Before defining who these people are, here's the actual market they're operating in. <br>In the year to February 2026, 50,688 dwelling purchases were filed with Revenue. Of these, 39.2% were first-time buyers, 50.8% were former owner-occupiers (movers/upsizers/downsizers), and 9.9% were non-occupiers (investors). The national median price was €390,000. The average age of a home buyer in 2024 was 40. The average income per transaction was €84,400. Nearly six in ten purchases were by couples. Over 27,000 first-time buyer mortgages were drawn in 2025 — the highest since 2008.

These aren't abstract segments. They're 50,000+ annual transactions, each one a person or couple who needed exactly the intelligence ProperData provides.

## 1. The Dublin escapee

**Who they are:** Couple, late 20s to late 30s, combined income €75-110K. Currently renting in Dublin at €2,000-2,800/month. Mortgage approval in principle for €300-400K. Priced out of Dublin where the median is €500K. One of them is on r/irishpersonalfinance. They've been outbid at least once.

**What happened:** They ran the numbers and realised that even with Help to Buy (€30K on a new build), Dublin is out of reach for the kind of home they want to raise a family in. A colleague mentioned they bought a 3-bed semi in Mullingar for €345K — less than a 2-bed apartment in Rathfarnham. They started looking at the M4/M6 corridor.

**Their emotional state:** Anxious, time-pressured, information-asymmetric. They know less than every other party in every transaction. Data is their levelling mechanism. They're terrified of overpaying. They're also excited — this might actually work.

**What they do at 2am:** Open Daft, see a new listing in Mullingar, and immediately wonder: Is this a good price? What did the house two doors down sell for? What's the commute really like? Is the broadband real fibre or "up to" marketing? What are the schools like? Is there planning for a massive housing estate behind the garden? What grants can I get?

**What ProperData gives them:**
- Comparable analysis: "This is 3.7% above the comp median — but 3 of the last 5 sales here sold over asking"
- Grant package: "You're eligible for Help to Buy (€30K if new build) plus SEAI grants of up to €23K if D-rated"
- Total cost of ownership: "Mortgage + LPT + insurance + energy + commute = €2,049/month vs your Dublin rent of €2,400"
- Digital commuter score: "SIRO fibre confirmed, 55 min M4 drive, 1hr 12min rail, 7 daily services"
- School proximity: "3 primary schools within 1km, all above-average pupil-teacher ratio"
- Planning alerts: "No large developments approved within 1km of this property"

**Lifecycle:** 6-18 months of intense usage, then they buy and either churn or transition to the homeowner persona. The grant calculator is the conversion trigger — "this tool told me I was eligible for €19K I didn't know about."

**Size of segment:** ~20,000 FTB purchases/year nationally. If even 2% of the Midlands/commuter belt subset subscribe: 200-400 subscribers.

**Willingness to pay:** €15/month during active search. 70% convert to annual after first month if they find value.

## 2. The spreadsheet investor

**Who they are:** Age 35-55, already owns a PPR. Has €50-200K deployable capital from savings, inheritance, or equity release. Reads Money Maximising Mums, AskPaul, or the property threads on Boards.ie. May already own 1-2 investment properties. Possibly Irish living abroad (US, UK, Australia, Gulf) wanting to invest "back home."

**What happened:** They've seen the returns from Irish property over the last decade (179% from the 2013 trough nationally) and want in — but they've also read about the regulatory minefield (RPZ rules, RZLT, tenant protection, 52% marginal tax on rental income). They want to model the numbers properly before committing capital.

**Their emotional state:** Analytical, patient, risk-aware. They don't buy on emotion — they buy on yield, adjusted for cost, tax, and regulatory risk. Their biggest frustration is that every Irish property "analysis" they find uses Daft asking rents instead of actual rents, ignores management costs, and doesn't factor in the landlord retrofit tax deduction.

**What they do at 2am:** Build a spreadsheet comparing yield across 6 towns, factoring in mortgage rates, management fees, void periods, and tax treatment. Then they second-guess the rent assumptions. Then they search "Mullingar rental yield 2026" and find nothing useful.

**What ProperData gives them:**
- Verified yield using RTB actual rents, not Daft asking prices
- Full cost model: mortgage at their specific rate, management fees, insurance, 4 weeks void, LPT, maintenance reserve
- Tax-adjusted return: landlord retrofit deduction (€10K × up to 3 properties), rental income at marginal rate
- Grant-adjusted acquisition: "Buy this D-rated 3-bed at €285K, retrofit with €22K in SEAI grants (net cost €14K), achieve B2, capture 12% BER premium on resale"
- Portfolio tracking: quarterly valuation update against PPR comps, yield recalculation on new RTB data
- Town comparison on investment-grade metrics: yield, liquidity (transaction volume), supply pressure (planning pipeline), capital growth trajectory, RZLT exposure
- Vacancy opportunity index: "Longford has the highest grant-to-price ratio in the coverage area — €70K Croí Cónaithe on a €180K vacant property"

**Lifecycle:** Ongoing, multi-year subscriber. The stickiest persona because the need is perpetual — they always want to know how their portfolio is performing and what to buy next. Annual billing is natural for this persona.

**Size of segment:** ~5,000 non-occupier purchases per year nationally. Plus ~100,000 existing landlords, many evaluating hold vs exit. Plus an unknown diaspora segment. If 2-3% of the Midlands subset subscribe: 100-200 subscribers, growing with national expansion.

**Willingness to pay:** €15/month (Insider) readily. Many would pay €49/month (Pro) for the API/export capability to feed their own models.

## 3. The anxious seller

**Who they are:** Age 40-65, owns a property they need to sell — downsizing, relocating, separating, or inheriting. May not have sold a property in 20+ years and has no idea what the current market looks like. Possibly going through a difficult life event (bereavement, divorce, financial pressure).

**What happened:** They need to sell but are terrified of underpricing (leaving money on the table) or overpricing (property sitting on the market, signalling desperation). The agent gave them a valuation but they want to verify it independently.

**Their emotional state:** Uncertain, sometimes vulnerable. Property is typically their largest asset. Getting the price wrong by 10% on a €350K property is a €35K mistake.

**What ProperData gives them:**
- Independent comparable analysis: "Based on 8 PPR sales within 2km in the last 12 months, fair value for your property is €345-370K. Your agent's guide of €365K is at the upper end of market — achievable in current conditions."
- BER premium context: "Your property is D-rated. If you invested €14.8K net (after SEAI grants) in a retrofit to B2, comparable sales suggest a 12-15% premium — potentially adding €42-52K to the sale price. ROI: 280-350%."
- Planning context: "No large developments approved nearby that would affect buyer demand"
- Market timing: "The heat score for Mullingar is 73/100 and rising. Transaction velocity is above the 12-month average. This is a seller-friendly window."
- Agent verification: "Your agent holds a valid PSRA Type B licence (sale of land), no sanctions on record"

**Lifecycle:** 3-6 months. Short, intense usage. But high willingness to pay because the stakes are enormous relative to subscription cost.

**Size of segment:** ~50,000 residential sales per year nationally. The seller is often less tech-savvy and harder to reach via Reddit/Twitter — LinkedIn and local newspaper advertising may be more effective.

**Willingness to pay:** €15/month for 3-6 months. A €90 total spend that could save €35K on a mispriced sale.

## 4. The reluctant landlord

**Who they are:** Age 40-60, owns 1-3 rental properties acquired during or after the crash (2010-2015). Originally saw property as a pension strategy. Now exhausted by regulation, tax, and tenant management. Seriously considering selling but unsure if the numbers justify holding.

**What happened:** Since they bought, the regulatory environment has transformed: RPZ caps on rent increases, extended notice periods, RTB dispute processes, RZLT on any zoned land they hold, 52% marginal tax on rental income, and now the March 2026 rent reset rules creating further complexity. They've heard 42,300 landlords exited between 2020-2025. They're wondering if they should join them.

**Their emotional state:** Frustrated, fatigued, but financially rational. They won't make a €300K+ decision based on emotion. They need the data to tell them: hold or sell?

**What ProperData gives them:**
- Hold-vs-sell analysis: "Your Mullingar property is yielding 4.7% gross (3.1% net after all costs and tax). Capital appreciation was +9.3% YoY. Total return: 12.4%. Selling crystallises a CGT liability of €X. Holding generates €X/year after tax. Break-even on selling vs holding is at year X."
- RTB Notices of Termination trend: "NoT velocity in Westmeath is increasing — landlords are exiting. This is reducing rental supply and pushing rents up, which improves your yield if you hold."
- Regulatory radar: "The March 2026 rent reset rules mean you cannot raise to market rate on re-let within an RPZ (which now covers Mullingar). But if your tenant leaves voluntarily, the new tenancy is at whatever rate the market will bear."
- Landlord retrofit tax deduction: "You can deduct up to €10K per property (max 3) from rental income for retrofit works, net of SEAI grants. On your 2 properties, that's €20K in tax deductions over 2026-2028."
- Grant-funded capital uplift: "Retrofit both properties to B2 using SEAI grants. Net cost: €29K. Estimated value uplift: €70-90K. If you decide to sell, you sell B2-rated stock that commands premium prices."

**Lifecycle:** 6-12 months of decision-making, then either sells (churns) or commits to holding (becomes the spreadsheet investor persona for ongoing monitoring).

**Size of segment:** Estimated 50,000-60,000 landlords with 1-3 properties nationally. The most emotionally charged segment — and the one most likely to share the product with other landlords in their network.

**Willingness to pay:** €15/month readily. This persona often graduates to €49/month Pro for the data exports they use with their accountant.

## 5. The professional who needs ammunition

**Who they are:** Auctioneer, estate agent, letting agent, mortgage broker, or conveyancing solicitor operating in the Midlands. Typically a 1-5 person firm. Competitive local market where winning a valuation instruction or mortgage referral can hinge on appearing more knowledgeable than the rival firm down the street.

**What happened:** A vendor is considering three agents. Agent A says "I think your house is worth around €350K." Agent B pulls out a branded ProperData report showing 8 PPR comparables within 2km, BER premium analysis, planning pipeline context, and a grant assessment: "Based on verified transaction data, your property's fair value range is €345-370K. Here's the evidence." Agent B gets the instruction.

**Their emotional state:** Time-poor, credibility-dependent. They don't use the product for their own decisions — they use it to look authoritative to clients. The product is a competitive weapon.

**What ProperData gives them:**
- Branded PDF reports: town-level and property-level, with the agent's logo and firm name. "Source: Property Price Register, SEAI BER Register, RTB/ESRI Rent Index" in the footer. Instant credibility.
- CSV data exports: importable into their own CMA tools or presentations
- All-town coverage: they need data on every property in their catchment, not just their personal interests
- Commercial lease intelligence: for agents handling mixed-use or commercial property
- Agent market analysis: how many licensed competitors in their area, what licence types they hold
- Conveyancing data pack (for solicitors): PPR comps + BER + flood risk + planning + RZLT + LPT + grant eligibility, per property, replacing hours of manual assembly

**Lifecycle:** Ongoing, multi-year subscriber. Once the data is embedded in their workflow (pulling reports for every valuation appointment), switching costs are real.

**Size of segment:** In the initial Midlands coverage area (Westmeath, Offaly, Laois, Longford): ~200-300 auctioneers/agents, ~300-400 property-active solicitors, ~100-200 mortgage brokers. Nationally: ~2,000-3,000 across all categories.

**Willingness to pay:** €49/month without hesitation — it's a business expense that pays for itself on a single instruction. The €449/year annual pricing is a no-brainer.

## 6. The returning emigrant

**Who they are:** Age 30-50, Irish-born, living abroad (US, UK, Australia, Middle East, mainland Europe) for 5-15 years. Seriously considering moving home — motivated by family, children's education, quality of life, or career opportunity. Has savings in foreign currency. Has no idea what has happened to Irish property prices since they left.

**What happened:** They left Ireland when you could buy a 3-bed semi in Mullingar for €180K. They're now seeing prices of €345K+ and wondering if they've missed the boat. They're also navigating the logistics of buying from abroad: mortgage approval as a non-resident, currency conversion, Help to Buy eligibility, which towns offer the best combination of value, schools, and connectivity.

**Their emotional state:** Nostalgic but pragmatic. They want to come home but they also understand money. They're comparing the cost of living (including property) in Ireland against their current location. They need data, not sentiment.

**What ProperData gives them:**
- Town comparison across all metrics they care about: price, schools, broadband, commute to likely employer hubs, healthcare, amenity access
- Help to Buy eligibility: "As a FTB purchasing a new build, you're eligible for up to €30K. You can claim against Irish income tax paid in the last 4 years, including tax paid before emigrating."
- First Home Scheme eligibility: "The shared equity scheme is available for qualifying new builds in Westmeath with a price ceiling of €XK"
- Grant package for older properties: "If you buy a second-hand property, you can't claim Help to Buy — but you can access SEAI grants of up to €23K. If the property is vacant 2+ years, add €50-70K Croí Cónaithe."
- Total cost of ownership comparison: "Your €X salary buys you €Y mortgage. In Mullingar, that gets you a 3-bed semi with €2,050/month total cost of ownership. In Dublin, the same monthly spend gets you a 2-bed apartment."
- Non-resident mortgage guidance: which Irish lenders offer mortgages to returning emigrants, typical requirements

**Lifecycle:** 6-18 months of research (often subscribing while still abroad), then either moves home (becomes a buyer persona) or decides against it (churns). High-value during the research phase because every piece of intelligence reduces the anxiety of making a life-changing decision from 5,000 miles away.

**Size of segment:** Hard to quantify precisely. There are estimated 1-3 million Irish-born people living abroad. The "seriously considering return" subset is probably 10,000-30,000 at any given time. If 1-2% subscribe: 100-600 subscribers. This segment is reachable via Irish diaspora Facebook groups, LinkedIn, and Irish media consumed abroad (RTÉ Player, The Irish Times, Irish Central).

**Willingness to pay:** €15/month, often annual upfront because the research phase is long and the commitment signals seriousness.

## 7. The renovation opportunist

**Who they are:** Age 30-55, either a FTB seeking a "fixer-upper" as a more affordable entry point, or an experienced renovator looking for the next project. Comfortable with construction risk. Thinks in terms of acquisition cost + renovation cost vs end value.

**What happened:** They saw the Croí Cónaithe grant headlines (€50-70K for vacant/derelict properties) and realised that combined with SEAI grants, the government is effectively subsidising acquisition and renovation. They want to find the right property where the numbers stack up.

**Their emotional state:** Entrepreneurial, numbers-driven. They're not buying a home — they're buying a project with a target margin. They need to model: purchase price + renovation cost - grants = net outlay, vs post-renovation value. If the spread is right, they move.

**What ProperData gives them:**
- Vacancy opportunity index: "These towns have the highest density of vacant properties with grant-eligible profiles"
- Grant-adjusted acquisition: "This derelict cottage in Longford at €120K qualifies for €70K Croí Cónaithe + €23K SEAI = €93K in grants. Effective cost: €27K. Post-renovation comparable value: €220-250K."
- Retrofit ROI calculator: "Net renovation cost after grants: €45K. Post-renovation value: €250K. Purchase at €120K. Total invested: €165K. Embedded equity: €85K. ROI: 52%."
- BER premium: "D-rated stock in this area sells for €X. B2-rated sells for €X. The spread is your margin."
- Planning check: "No restrictions on this site. Irish Water capacity confirmed. No protected structure designation."

**Lifecycle:** Ongoing if they're serial renovators (project every 2-3 years). Short-term if they're doing one project. The vacancy opportunity index is the killer feature for this persona — it's a deal-finding tool.

**Size of segment:** Niche but growing rapidly due to the Croí Cónaithe grant programme. Probably 1,000-3,000 active renovation-focused buyers/investors nationally. High engagement, high share rate ("you need to see what this tool found").

**Willingness to pay:** €15/month (Insider), often Pro (€49/month) for data exports used in planning applications and bank presentations.

## 8. The land or site owner

**Who they are:** Age 40-70, owns zoned residential land or a site — inherited, purchased speculatively, or attached to a farm. Now faces the RZLT (3% annual tax on market value from 2026) and needs to decide: develop, sell, or seek rezoning.

**What happened:** The RZLT maps were published on 31 January 2026 and their land appeared on them. They're now liable for 3% of market value annually. A €500K site costs €15,000/year in RZLT. They have until 1 April 2026 to apply for rezoning, or until 23 May 2026 to file a return and pay.

**Their emotional state:** Shocked (many didn't expect to be mapped), confused (the exemption and rezoning rules are complex), and under time pressure (deadlines are imminent).

**What ProperData gives them:**
- RZLT map overlay: "Your land parcel is on the 2026 final map. Here's the exact area identified."
- Market value assessment: "Based on PPR land sales within 5km, comparable zoned residential land is trading at €X per hectare. Your RZLT liability is estimated at €X/year."
- Development feasibility: "Irish Water capacity register shows adequate wastewater capacity for your area. Planning pipeline shows 3 comparable schemes approved within 2km in the last 24 months."
- Option modelling: "Option A: Develop (planning risk, construction cost, 18-36 month timeline, but PPR comps suggest end value of €X). Option B: Sell to a developer (comparable land sales suggest €X). Option C: Apply for rezoning (deadline 1 April 2026, outcome by 30 June 2026)."
- Grant intelligence: "If you develop, the Residential Development Stamp Duty Refund Scheme allows you to recover up to two-thirds of the stamp duty paid on the land purchase."

**Lifecycle:** Acute (immediate RZLT deadline), then ongoing if they decide to develop (planning + construction monitoring). Or one-time if they sell.

**Size of segment:** Hard to quantify. The RZLT maps cover thousands of hectares nationally. Dún Laoghaire-Rathdown alone has 3,295 hectares mapped. Many landowners are farmers, inheritors, or speculators who've held sites for years. Probably 5,000-10,000 affected landowners nationally. This is a premium advisory opportunity (€200-500 per RZLT assessment) rather than a mass subscription play.

**Willingness to pay:** High for one-off assessment (€200-500). Lower for ongoing subscription unless they decide to develop (then Pro tier at €49/month for planning pipeline monitoring).

## 9. The local authority planner

**Who they are:** Planning officer, housing officer, or development plan team member in a county or city council. Responsible for housing strategy, development plan reviews, RZLT mapping, or Housing Need and Demand Assessment (HNDA).

**What happened:** They need data to support policy decisions — how many homes are needed, where should they be zoned, what's the price/supply/demand dynamic in their area? They currently commission expensive consultant studies (€10-50K) for this analysis, or spend weeks assembling it manually from PPR, Census, and planning data.

**Their emotional state:** Overwhelmed by data demand, under-resourced, under political pressure to show they're addressing housing needs.

**What ProperData gives them:**
- Town-level housing market assessment: PPR price trends, transaction volumes, supply pipeline, vacancy rates, BER stock profile, demographic demand — pre-assembled and updated automatically
- RZLT intelligence: which mapped sites are developable (Irish Water capacity) vs constrained, what development applications have been filed on RZLT land
- Affordable housing demand modelling: Census income data vs PPR prices vs Central Bank mortgage rules = how many households can afford to buy in each town, and the gap between supply and demand
- Planning pipeline monitoring: what's coming through ePlanning in their area, residential unit counts, approval/refusal rates

**Lifecycle:** Ongoing if the council subscribes. Annual contract or per-report pricing.

**Size of segment:** 31 local authorities nationally. Each has 3-10 relevant staff. This is an Enterprise-tier customer at €500-2,000/month per council — or a white-label product sold through planning consultancies.

**Willingness to pay:** High (institutional budgets), but long procurement cycles. Better approached through existing planning consultancy relationships.

## 10. The mortgage broker or financial advisor

**Who they are:** Authorised mortgage intermediary or financial advisor. They advise clients on mortgage products, property investment, and financial planning. Their value to clients is being more informed than the client about the market.

**What happened:** A client walks in and says "I'm thinking of buying a 3-bed in Mullingar for €345K. Is that a good price? What would the repayments be? What grants can I get?" The broker currently answers the mortgage question well but has no data on whether the price is fair, what grants apply, or how the total cost of ownership compares to renting in Dublin.

**What ProperData gives them:**
- Pre-consultation intelligence: pull a town report before the client meeting. Know the median prices, recent sales, and market dynamics.
- Grant-aware affordability: "Your client is eligible for Help to Buy (€30K) + SEAI grants (€19K). Effective acquisition cost: €296K. Mortgage required: €266K at 90% LTV. Monthly repayment: €1,210."
- Total cost of ownership: assemble the full monthly cost picture as part of the mortgage advice conversation
- Branded reports: white-labelled with the broker's firm name, given to clients as a value-add that differentiates them from competitors
- BER-linked mortgage intelligence: "Green mortgages from AIB start at 3.0% for BER A1-B3. This D-rated property wouldn't qualify — but after SEAI-funded retrofit, it would. The rate saving is €X/month."

**Lifecycle:** Ongoing (business subscription). Brokers see 10-50 clients/month. If the data improves their conversion rate or average deal size, the subscription pays for itself instantly.

**Size of segment:** ~2,500 authorised mortgage intermediaries in Ireland. Plus ~1,500 financial advisors who handle property-related advice.

**Willingness to pay:** €49/month (Pro) without hesitation. It's a cost-of-doing-business tool. White-label options at €79-149/month for larger brokerages.
